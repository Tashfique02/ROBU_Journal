01 Path to Automation (LFR Workshop)
Organized by the Arts & Design and Technical teams, this hands-on workshop equips students with fundamental robotics concepts, sensor calibration (IR arrays), motor drivers (L298N/TB6612FNG), PID control loops, and microcontroller programming for smart Line-Following Robots.
02 Soccer Bot Workshop
Hosted by the Event Management Department with expert guest instructors, focusing on mechanical chassis design, wireless RF/Bluetooth communication, differential drive mechanics, and tactical strategy for competitive 1v1 robotic soccer tournaments.
03 LaTeX for Research & Academic Resumes
Conducted by senior club researchers and alumni, utilizing Overleaf to train members in typesetting IEEE paper templates, automated citation management (BibTeX), mathematical formulation, and high-impact CV formatting.
04 GitHub & Career Optimization Bootcamp
Foundational bootcamps on Git version control, branch management, open-source pull requests, alongside HR-led professional development seminars covering LinkedIn networking, portfolio design, and technical interview preparation.
05 Embedded Hardware & Firmware Engineering
An intensive industrial-grade program delving into STM32 32-bit ARM Cortex microcontrollers, bare-metal C programming, RTOS task scheduling, UART/SPI/I2C communication protocols, and PCB schematic design in EasyEDA/KiCAD.
06 Introduction to ROS & Autonomous Navigation (RPM Workshop)

Organized by the Research and Project Management (RPM) Department in Fall ’25, this workshop guides recruits through fundamental Robot Operating System (ROS) architecture, covering ROS nodes, topics, services, workspaces, Gazebo 3D simulation environments, and TurtleBot sensor data processing for autonomous navigation behaviors.

Participant Spotlight: "My Journey Through the Introduction to ROS Workshop"

"I’m incredibly grateful to have been selected as the winner of the Introduction to ROS Workshop task organized by the Research and Project Management department in Fall ‘25.

As a complete beginner in ROS, I was overwhelmed with concepts like ROS nodes, topics, services, workspaces, and TurtleBot sensors when I started out. Struggling to understand the TurtleBot documentation and sensor data, I wanted to give up many times. The final task felt quite tough, but I’m glad I pushed through and completed the entire thing.

One of the most struggling points during this task was the final hours. As I was nearing the end and trying to put everything together, my laptop kept crashing while trying to run the Gazebo simulation. Each time, I restarted my system and tested the code again—navigating through VirtualBox and Gazebo simulation workloads.

I struggled with the code quite a bit, too. Although I was comfortable with object-oriented programming in Java, I found myself having trouble applying the same in Python. I searched up tutorials online and accessed resources provided by the instructor. At the end, I was able to implement an autonomous navigation behavior on TurtleBot through the integration of ROS nodes and topics.

Despite the difficulties, I had a lot of fun doing the final task and got to learn a lot of things. I’m truly grateful for the opportunity to have learnt ROS in such a supportive and encouraging environment."

Corporate-Style Progression Track

At ROBU, every new recruit embarks on a structured leadership roadmap. You begin by learning technical & organizational fundamentals and systematically climb to executive panel management.

1. Apprentice (AP)

Entry-level recruit stage. Hands-on learning through departmental bootcamps, shadow assignments, and workshop participation.

2. Executive Body

Progression through Junior Executive → Executive → Senior Executive. Operational ownership over event execution, software tools, design, and budget management.

3. Sub-Panel Leadership

Roles: Secretary → Assistant Director (AD) → Director. Strategic leadership over functional departments (SP, F&M, HR, EM, A&D, IT, E&P, RPM).

4. Core Panel & Advisory Board

Highest executive leadership: President, Vice President, General Secretary, guided by the Faculty Advisor. Directing club strategy, global sponsorships, and university relations.

Event Report · Innovation & AI

Codex event pulled off successfully

By Humayra Saniat Lithe

Three hours of hands-on exploration turned ideas into working prototypes—and brought innovation, collaboration, and learning together.

Codex: BuildCave successfully brought together innovation, collaboration, and hands-on learning in an engaging three-hour interactive experience, powered by Microsoft Build and Qwen.

The session began with an immersive Microsoft Build hands-on lab, where participants explored Microsoft's development ecosystem through guided practical exercises. This was followed by an exciting agentic AI-driven development session where participants experienced the power of AI-assisted development and transformed ideas into working prototypes using the provided API credits.

Throughout the event, participants actively engaged in building, experimenting, and collaborating, creating an energetic learning environment filled with curiosity and creativity. The enthusiasm and dedication shown by everyone made the session both productive and inspiring.

The event concluded on a high note as participants showcased their builds, with the top projects earning $100 Codex API Credits in recognition of their creativity and technical execution.

A huge thank you to everyone who joined us and contributed to making Codex: BuildCave a successful and memorable experience. We also extend our sincere gratitude to Microsoft Build and Qwen for powering the event and supporting hands-on AI learning.

Here's to building more, learning more, and innovating together.

Expo Feature · Local Innovation

Broadband Expo held successfully by ROBU

By Nabil Wasit

More than a showcase, the expo reflected an innovation ecosystem in motion—and the student teams bringing it to life.

At the Broadband Expo hosted by the ISP Association of Bangladesh, a quiet but compelling story of innovation unfolded over three days—one that spoke not only of machines and prototypes, but of persistence, vision, and a growing national confidence in homegrown technology. The Robotics Club of BRAC University successfully showcased its talents and outstanding projects to the audience.

The initiative to participate in the expo was undertaken by ROBU, whose members curated and presented a cross-section of one of the country's longest-running student-driven robotics ecosystems. What they brought to the exhibition floor was not just a display, but a narrative built over 15 years. They have been constantly surpassing their limits to generate innovative ideas and exceptional performance.

A major feature of the event was the presence of Zahir Uddin Swapon, Honorable Minister of Information and Broadcasting, alongside Rehan Asif Asad, Advisor to the Prime Minister. Their visit added both recognition and encouragement to what is, at its core, a student-led journey. It underscored a growing alignment between policy, education, and innovation. The honorable minister highly praised and appreciated the projects undertaken and made by passionate young students of ROBU.

At the heart of the showcase was BRAC University's robotics ecosystem, an effort that has evolved steadily through years of experimentation, mentorship, and relentless effort. What began as a modest initiative has matured into a platform that not only builds machines but also nurtures talent and ideas. Here, ROBU proved that it can accomplish the best with its remarkable talents and hard work.

Autonomous and semi-autonomous systems such as BRACU Duburi, BRACU Mongol-Tori, and BRACU Ognibir drew significant attention, alongside service-oriented restaurant robots that demonstrated practical, everyday applications of robotics. Each system reflected a different approach to solving real-world challenges, from exploration to automation. They all serve an important purpose in the technological aspects of Bangladesh's IT sectors.

For many visitors, the experience was unexpectedly revealing. A recurring sentiment echoed across conversations: surprise. There remains a widespread perception that cutting-edge robotics belongs elsewhere. The exhibition challenged that assumption. Here was clear evidence that advanced research and development is taking place within Bangladesh, driven by students who are not only learning but actively contributing to technological progress.

By the third day, the exhibition settled into a more conversational rhythm. Without the formality of high-profile visits, deeper discussions began to take shape. Industry representatives, academics, and visitors engaged closely with the teams, leading to multiple expressions of interest in collaboration. These interactions pointed toward a promising bridge between academic innovation and industry application.

What emerged over the three days was more than a showcase; it was a reflection of an ecosystem in motion. Behind each project stands a network of dedicated individuals who have invested years of effort, often working beyond formal hours, to bring ideas into reality. The impact of this work is already visible, with startups emerging from the ecosystem and its influence continuing to expand. ROBU ensured its work was highly evaluated and checked by the best critics.

Yet perhaps the most important takeaway lies in visibility. For all its progress, much of this work remains underrepresented in public discourse. Platforms like the Broadband Expo offer a crucial opportunity to tell these stories, make visible what is being built, and inspire a wider audience. This event helped indirectly advertise the work and created a medium to interact face-to-face with policymakers, elected officials, and the general public.

There is a growing sense that such initiatives should not be occasional, but regular. Showcasing local innovation, fostering dialogue, and connecting ecosystems with industry are not just beneficial; they are necessary. If Bangladesh is to fully realize its technological potential, it must continue to create and support more spaces where ideas can be shared openly and achievements can be seen and understood.

In this sense, the ROBU effort went beyond simply attending an expo. It served as a reminder that the nation's innovation story is already being written and should be shared more frequently and more loudly.

Competition Feature · Global Achievement

Duburi secures 8th position globally in ROBOSUB

By Mohammed Tashfiqul Islam

A global top-10 finish—and second place in Asia—marks another milestone for BRACU Duburi and Bangladeshi robotics.

The Robotics Club of BRAC University extends its sincere congratulations to BRACU Duburi for securing 8th position globally (2nd in Asia) in the ROBOSUB 2025 worldwide UAV Championship. The team also proudly represented Bangladesh by securing 3rd place for its team website, 5th place for its introductory video, and 6th place in the actual assessments.

Duburi's achievements are a trophy in themselves: a source of motivation for young innovators. As the first of its kind in Bangladesh, reaching the world's top 10 in one of the largest UAV competitions—and doing so twice—has been a living inspiration. Its story reaches beyond universities, making a mark in entrepreneurship through Dubotech, a UAV startup founded by the alumni who helped make Duburi known around the world.

DuboMini, the Duburi team's latest creation, is an assistant UAV to the enhanced Duburi 4.2. It took the stage with its inter-bot signal transmission, demonstrating coordinated communication in the underwater arena. With remarkable efficiency, the achievement felt like capturing the moment through a fish-eye lens.

We once again congratulate the entire DUBURI team for overcoming every obstacle and leading the way for Bangladesh on the global stage. It is an absolute honour to recognize their work, effort, and outstanding performance in representing our country, our university, and all of us.
